1. Definitions

In this DPA, unless the context indicates otherwise, the following terms bear the meanings assigned to them in POPIA and as supplemented below:

  1. "Data Subject" means a natural person whose personal information is processed under this DPA, including learners, assessors, and administrative users of the Platform.
  2. "Information Regulator" means the body established under section 39 of POPIA.
  3. "Operator" means a person who processes personal information for a responsible party in terms of a contract or mandate, without coming under the direct authority of that responsible party, as contemplated in section 1 of POPIA.
  4. "Personal Information" has the meaning set out in section 1 of POPIA, and includes for the purposes of this DPA the categories of information described in Schedule A.
  5. "Platform" means the NQF Learner Placement Assessment Platform, a web-based software application operated by the Operator on behalf of the Responsible Party.
  6. "POPIA" means the Protection of Personal Information Act 4 of 2013, as amended.
  7. "Processing" has the meaning set out in section 1 of POPIA and includes any operation performed on personal information, whether automated or not.
  8. "Responsible Party" means a public or private body or any other person which, alone or in conjunction with others, determines the purpose of and means for processing personal information, as contemplated in section 1 of POPIA.
  9. "Security Compromise" means the unauthorised access to, acquisition of, use of, disclosure of, modification of, or destruction of personal information.
  10. "Sub-operator" means any third party engaged by the Operator to process personal information on behalf of the Responsible Party, as set out in Schedule B.

2. Scope and Purpose of Processing

  1. The Operator processes personal information solely for the purpose of providing the Platform and related services to the Responsible Party as described in the Principal Agreement and in Schedule A to this DPA.
  2. The Operator shall not process personal information for any purpose other than that instructed by the Responsible Party, unless required to do so by law, in which case the Operator shall notify the Responsible Party of such requirement prior to processing, unless prohibited by law from doing so.
  3. The Responsible Party warrants that it has a lawful basis, as contemplated in Chapter 3 of POPIA, for collecting and transferring the personal information to the Operator for processing.
  4. The Responsible Party remains the Responsible Party in respect of all personal information processed under this DPA. The Operator acts solely as Operator and does not acquire any rights over the personal information beyond those strictly necessary to perform its obligations.

3. Obligations of the Operator

The Operator shall:

  1. process personal information only on documented instructions from the Responsible Party, including with regard to transfers of personal information to a foreign country or an international organisation;
  2. ensure that all persons authorised to process the personal information have committed themselves to confidentiality obligations no less protective than those set out in section 54 of POPIA;
  3. implement and maintain appropriate technical and organisational security measures as required under section 19 of POPIA and as further described in Schedule C;
  4. not engage a Sub-operator without prior written authorisation from the Responsible Party, and ensure that any Sub-operator is bound by data processing obligations equivalent to those set out in this DPA;
  5. assist the Responsible Party in responding to requests from Data Subjects exercising their rights under POPIA (including rights of access, correction, and objection to processing), insofar as this is reasonably possible given the nature of the processing;
  6. assist the Responsible Party in ensuring compliance with its obligations under sections 19 through 22 of POPIA (security of processing and notification of compromises);
  7. at the election of the Responsible Party, delete or return all personal information upon termination of the Principal Agreement, and delete existing copies unless retention is required by applicable law;
  8. make available to the Responsible Party all information reasonably necessary to demonstrate compliance with the obligations set out in this DPA, and permit audits as set out in clause 8.

4. Security of Processing

  1. The Operator shall implement technical and organisational measures appropriate to the risk presented by the processing, having regard to the nature of the personal information processed. The specific measures in place at the effective date of this DPA are described in Schedule C.
  2. The Operator shall ensure that access to personal information is restricted to personnel who require access for the performance of their duties and who are bound by appropriate confidentiality obligations.
  3. The Operator shall maintain access control mechanisms, including password protection, role-based access controls, and session management controls, to prevent unauthorised access to the Platform and the personal information held therein.
  4. The Operator shall maintain an immutable audit log of all score changes, including the identity of the user who made the change, the time of change, and the values before and after the change. This log shall be accessible to authorised staff of the Responsible Party on request.
  5. The Platform shall enforce rate limiting and account lockout mechanisms to mitigate brute-force attacks on authentication endpoints.

5. Security Compromise Notification

  1. In the event of a Security Compromise involving personal information processed under this DPA, the Operator shall notify the Responsible Party without undue delay and, where feasible, within 72 hours of becoming aware of the compromise.
  2. Such notification shall, at a minimum, include:
    • a description of the nature of the Security Compromise, including the categories and approximate number of Data Subjects and records affected;
    • the name and contact details of the Operator's contact person from whom further information may be obtained;
    • a description of the likely consequences of the Security Compromise; and
    • a description of the measures taken or proposed to be taken to address the Security Compromise, including measures to mitigate its possible adverse effects.
  3. The Responsible Party shall be responsible for notifying the Information Regulator and affected Data Subjects as required by section 22 of POPIA. The Operator shall cooperate fully with the Responsible Party in fulfilling these obligations.
  4. Notification by the Operator under clause 5.1 shall be made to the Responsible Party's designated contact at the address specified in the Principal Agreement.

6. Data Subject Rights

  1. The Responsible Party is the primary point of contact for Data Subjects exercising their rights under POPIA. The Operator shall not respond directly to Data Subject requests without the prior written authorisation of the Responsible Party, except where required by law.
  2. Upon receiving a written request from the Responsible Party, the Operator shall, within a reasonable time and at the Responsible Party's expense where the request imposes unreasonable cost on the Operator:
    • provide a copy of any personal information held in respect of a specified Data Subject;
    • correct, update, or delete personal information in respect of a specified Data Subject; and
    • restrict the processing of personal information in respect of a specified Data Subject pending resolution of a dispute as to its accuracy.

7. Transborder Flows of Personal Information

  1. The Operator hosts the Platform and its database on infrastructure provided by Render Services, Inc., a United States-based cloud provider, as described in Schedule B. Personal information processed under this DPA is stored on servers which may be located outside the Republic of South Africa.
  2. The Operator warrants that such transborder transfer is conducted subject to adequate safeguards consistent with section 72 of POPIA, and that the recipient of such information is bound by a law, binding corporate rules, or a binding agreement that provides an adequate level of protection substantially similar to the conditions for the lawful processing of personal information under POPIA.
  3. The Responsible Party consents to the transborder transfer of personal information described in this clause for the purpose of providing the Platform services.

8. Audit and Inspection

  1. The Operator shall, upon not less than 10 business days' written notice, make available to the Responsible Party (or an auditor mandated by the Responsible Party) all information, records, and access reasonably necessary to verify the Operator's compliance with its obligations under this DPA.
  2. The Responsible Party shall conduct audits no more than once per calendar year unless a Security Compromise has occurred or there is reasonable cause to believe a material breach of this DPA has occurred.
  3. Any auditor appointed by the Responsible Party shall be bound by obligations of confidentiality no less protective than those in this DPA.
  4. The cost of any audit shall be borne by the Responsible Party unless the audit reveals a material breach by the Operator, in which case the Operator shall bear reasonable audit costs.

9. Retention and Deletion of Personal Information

  1. The Operator shall retain personal information only for so long as is necessary to fulfil the purposes described in Schedule A, or as required by applicable law or the Principal Agreement.
  2. The default retention periods applicable to each category of personal information are set out in Schedule A. The Responsible Party may instruct shorter retention periods in writing at any time.
  3. Upon termination or expiry of the Principal Agreement, the Operator shall, within 30 calendar days of written request by the Responsible Party:
    • return to the Responsible Party a complete export of all personal information held in the Platform in a standard machine-readable format (CSV or JSON); and
    • securely delete all remaining personal information from the Platform's database and backups, except to the extent that retention is required by applicable law.
  4. The Operator shall provide written confirmation of deletion upon completion.

10. Sub-operators

  1. The Responsible Party hereby grants general written authorisation for the Operator to engage the Sub-operators listed in Schedule B. The Operator shall inform the Responsible Party of any intended addition or replacement of Sub-operators, giving the Responsible Party a reasonable opportunity to object.
  2. The Operator shall ensure that each Sub-operator is bound by a written data processing agreement imposing obligations no less protective than those set out in this DPA.
  3. The Operator remains fully liable to the Responsible Party for the performance of each Sub-operator's obligations under their respective data processing agreements.

11. Term and Termination

  1. This DPA shall come into effect on the Effective Date and shall continue in force for the duration of the Principal Agreement.
  2. Termination of the Principal Agreement shall automatically terminate this DPA. Clauses 4, 5, 9, and 12 shall survive termination.

12. Liability

  1. Each party shall be liable for any damage caused to Data Subjects or third parties as a result of that party's breach of this DPA or POPIA.
  2. The Operator's liability under this DPA shall be subject to the limitations of liability set out in the Principal Agreement, except in the case of wilful misconduct or gross negligence by the Operator.
  3. Nothing in this DPA shall limit either party's liability to Data Subjects or the Information Regulator.

13. Governing Law

This DPA shall be governed by and construed in accordance with the laws of the Republic of South Africa. Any dispute arising from or in connection with this DPA shall be resolved in accordance with the dispute resolution provisions of the Principal Agreement.

Schedule C — Technical and Organisational Security Measures

Access Control

  • Role-based access control: learner, assessor, and staff roles with distinct permission sets
  • Session-bound attempt ownership: each learner attempt is bound to the browser session that created it, preventing cross-learner navigation
  • All assessor and staff routes protected by authenticated session requirement
  • Questions view restricted to staff only

Authentication Security

  • Failed login rate limiting: maximum 5 failed attempts per 10-minute window per IP address, enforced server-side via Django signal
  • Brute-force lockout mechanism with configurable threshold
  • Django's built-in PBKDF2 password hashing with SHA-256
  • CSRF protection enabled on all state-changing requests

Data Integrity

  • Submitted attempts are locked against further modification
  • Score audit log records every score creation and modification with before/after values, assessor identity, mode (auto/manual), and timestamp
  • Unique constraints on attempt-question response pairs prevent duplicate records

Infrastructure

  • Application served over HTTPS (TLS 1.2+) enforced at the hosting layer
  • Database credentials, secret keys, and environment configuration managed via environment variables; not present in source code
  • Django DEBUG=False in production; detailed error pages suppressed
  • Managed PostgreSQL with automated daily backups provided by Render (7-day retention on paid plans)

Incident Response

  • Security compromises to be reported to the Responsible Party within 72 hours of discovery as set out in clause 5
