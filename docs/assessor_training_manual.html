1. Logging In

Navigate to your platform URL in any modern desktop browser (Chrome, Edge, or Firefox recommended).

Enter your username and password, then click Sign in.

You will land on the Assessor Dashboard, which shows a summary of recent activity: attempts in progress, submitted, and awaiting marking.

Important Do not share your login credentials with learners or anyone who should not have assessor access. Each assessor must have their own account.

Forgotten password

On the login page, click Forgot password? and enter your email address. A reset link valid for 1 hour will be sent to you. If no email arrives, check your spam folder or contact support@oanagakara.co.za.

Adding a new assessor account

You do not need to contact the platform administrator to add a new assessor. Generate a single-use invite link directly from the platform:

  1. Sign in and click Invite Assessor in the left navigation bar.
  2. Click Generate invite link.
  3. Copy the link and send it to the new assessor (Teams, email, etc.).
  4. The new assessor opens the link and completes the registration form.
  5. Their account is created automatically with assessor access.
Note Each invite link is single-use and expires after 48 hours. If the link expires before the assessor registers, generate a new one.

2. Before the Session

2.1 Create a Session

  1. Click Sessions in the left navigation bar.
  2. Click New session.
  3. Select the assessment template from the dropdown.
  4. Click Create session.

A unique session code is generated (e.g., AB12CD34). This is what learners will enter to join.

Timing note Sessions expire 2 hours after creation. Do not create the session more than 15 minutes before learners are expected to arrive.

2.2 Share the Join URL and Session Code

Give learners the platform URL and the session code. They do not need a username or password; the session code is their entry point.

The join flow takes approximately 2 minutes:

  1. Enter session code
  2. Enter personal details (name, ID number, etc.)
  3. Accept honesty declaration
  4. Assessment begins

3. Running the Session

3.1 The Assessment Structure

The NQF Literacy Placement Assessment — General consists of two timed sections:

SectionContentTime limit
Section 1Literacy — NQF Level 1–260 minutes
Section 2Literacy — NQF Level 3–460 minutes

A countdown timer is visible to the learner at all times. When a section's time expires, the learner is automatically moved to the next section. Any unanswered questions in the expired section receive zero marks.

After both sections are complete, learners may review their answers (90 seconds per question) or submit immediately.

3.2 Monitor Progress

The Session monitor shows a live grid of all seats. It refreshes every 30 seconds.

Seat colourMeaning
OrangeLearner is in progress
GreenLearner has submitted
RedAttempt is incomplete (learner did not submit)
White (dashed)Seat is empty / not yet joined

Each card shows the learner's name, current question, a progress bar, and time elapsed.

Browser notifications The platform will ask permission to send browser notifications when you first open the session monitor. Allow this. You will receive a notification each time a learner submits, and a final notification when all seats have submitted.

3.3 Learner Cannot Continue

If a learner's computer crashes or they leave without submitting, their attempt will appear Incomplete (red) after the session expires. This is expected. Incomplete attempts can still be marked; navigate to Attempts → Incomplete to find them. If the learner needs to re-sit, create a new session.

4. After the Session: Marking and Finalisation

4.1 The Review Queue

After learners submit, navigate to Review Queue in the left navigation. This lists every attempt that still has questions requiring your input.

The badge number next to "Review Queue" shows how many attempts are pending. This number decreases as you mark.

Click Mark → next to an attempt to begin.

4.2 Handwritten Essay Submissions

Some learners choose to handwrite their essay response rather than type it. When this happens, collect the handwritten page from the learner when they submit and upload it before marking.

  1. In the marking view, find the Writing submission card.
  2. Click Upload.
  3. Select the scan or photo from your device. (Accepted: JPEG, PNG, PDF)
  4. The file uploads immediately. Click View → to open it alongside the scoring form when marking.
If the page was lost Note "Handwritten submission not available" in the assessor note field and score based on any typed content provided.

4.3 Navigating Questions

The sidebar on the left of the marking view lists every question requiring your input:

  • Orange dot = still needs marking
  • Green dot = done (you can still click to revise)

Click any question to jump directly to it.

4.4 Scoring a Question

Each question shows three panels:

  • Left top: The question prompt
  • Left bottom: The learner's typed response
  • Right: The scoring form

If a rubric is shown:
Enter marks per criterion. An optional note field is available per criterion for written feedback.

If no rubric (manual mark):
Enter a number in the "Points out of X" field. Use the assessor note field for written comments.

Marking guidance Many questions show a blue Marking guidance box with the expected answer or scoring criteria. Always read this before awarding marks.

4.6 The Marking Summary

After all questions are scored, the Summary screen shows a table of every question with:

  • Awarded marks
  • Maximum marks
  • Status: Done / Pending / Unscored

Use the Edit links to return to any question and correct a score.

Finalising an attempt

When you are satisfied with all marks, click Finalise (the green button at the bottom right of the Summary screen). Finalising locks the attempt: scores cannot be changed by an assessor after this point.

After finalising you are returned to the Marked tab in Attempts. The attempt moves out of the pending count and is now available for moderator review if your organisation uses that workflow.

Finalise is the point of no return for assessors. After you click Finalise, you cannot change any mark. If you need to correct a score on a finalised attempt, contact your moderator or platform administrator. Do not finalise until you are confident in all marks.
Still have pending questions? You can click Finalise even if some questions are still showing as Pending. However, pending questions will remain unscored. Verify you have no pending questions before clicking Finalise.

4.7 Auto-Marked Questions

The platform automatically scores most question types. Auto-marked scores appear:

  • In the sidebar under Audit log (collapsed by default; staff only)
  • In the auto-marked results view, accessible from the attempt's row in the Attempts list

You do not need to re-enter auto-marked scores. If you disagree with an auto-mark, open the question from the marking view sidebar and save a manual override before finalising.

5. Results and Reporting

5.1 Viewing Results

Navigate to Results in the left navigation. All marked attempts appear here, sorted by most recent first. Incomplete attempts that have been marked are included and shown with an Incomplete badge so you can distinguish them from fully submitted attempts.

Each row shows:

  • Attempt code (click to open the full marking view)
  • Date submitted
  • Learner name and ID number
  • Per-group literacy scores
  • NQF level achieved for literacy
  • NQF placement recommendation

5.2 Searching and Filtering

Use the search bar at the top of the Results page to filter by:

  • Code: partial match on attempt code
  • Learner name: searches first name and surname
  • Date: filter to a specific date

Click Clear to remove all filters.

5.3 Exporting Data

Click Export CSV on the Results page to download a spreadsheet of all attempt scores. This file can be opened in Excel or Google Sheets.

Timer ran out before learner finished a section This is expected behaviour. Unanswered questions receive zero marks automatically
Review Queue badge shows 0 but you have unmarked attempts All auto-markable questions are done. Check the Attempts list for attempts without full scores

Handwritten essay page was lost Note "Handwritten submission not available" in the assessor note field and score based on any typed content available
Learner disputes a score Retrieve the attempt from the Attempts list and review the response and your assessor note

Appendix A: Literacy Level Grading

The literacy assessment is divided into four parts. Each part is scored independently and assigned an NQF level based on the mark achieved as a percentage of that part's total. The learner's overall literacy placement is the lowest level achieved across all four parts — a weak score on any one part, including writing, gates the final result.

NQF L1 Below basic competency
NQF L2 Basic competency
NQF L3 Intermediate competency
NQF L4 Competent
Part Description Max NQF L1 NQF L2 NQF L3 NQF L4
GEN-AReading & Comprehension A11 0 – 45 – 67 – 89 – 11
GEN-BReading & Comprehension B10 0 – 34 – 56 – 78 – 10
GEN-CReading & Comprehension C17 0 – 67 – 1011 – 1314 – 17
GEN-DLanguage & Usage12 0 – 45 – 78 – 910 – 12
GEN-EReading & Comprehension E30 0 – 1112 – 1718 – 2324 – 30
GEN-FReading & Comprehension F20 0 – 78 – 1112 – 1516 – 20
GEN-GWriting (assessor-marked)8 0 – 345 – 67 – 8
Total marks 108 0 – 39% 40 – 59% 60 – 79% 80 – 100%

The total percentage row is a reference only. The final placement is determined by the lowest individual part level, not the overall percentage.

Example A learner scores L4 on GEN-A through GEN-F, but L2 on GEN-G (Writing). Their overall literacy placement is NQF L2.
GEN-G is the only manually marked part. All other parts (GEN-A through GEN-F) are auto-marked. GEN-G (Writing) goes to the review queue and must be scored before the attempt can be finalised.
